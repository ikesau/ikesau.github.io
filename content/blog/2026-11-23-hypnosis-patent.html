--- title: hypnosis patent tags: - computers - essay ---

Google has filed for a patent that would use the information it knows about you to create an AI-generated TV channel full of personalized “shows” starring you and featuring customized advertisements. The patent is the latest signal of the AI video endgame: endless highly specific, customized content that is automatically generated based on the wealth of data companies and advertisers collect about you.

Jason Koebler, 404 media

some thoughts:

The companies all realize that if they don't do this, the others will. The technology to make the videos exists. The techniques for finding out how to maximize engagement are known. They've all got their nasty little TikTok clones and if one of them doesn't do this, they'll lose.

I'm sure it will work in the sense that people would watch it. For some fraction of the audience it could even be highly edifying. But the companies' incentives are not to help their audience improve themselves. They want them to watch as much as possible. Engagement line must go up.

As long as it's legal, they'll compete to make their platforms as addicting as possible. They won't try to make it easy for their audience to only watch 30 minutes of high quality video a day.

I don't think the market is capable of solving this. "Ethical social media" platforms like the Fediverse exist, but my sense is that the majority of their users are there for idealistic reasons. Mass idealism is not a quality you can easily manifest with arguments. Most people, when given the choice between chocolate and chickpeas, pick chocolate.

The issue is then that the political economy of regulating social media companies in the US is nearly impossible. The tech companies have massive war chests, and the first amendment is an unusually strong defence against many laws that would try to restrict such things. I guess there have been successful bans around the world for phones in schools, but once kids turn 18, they'd be vulnerable. And not just them, adults of all ages.

Maybe these lawsuits against compulsive design stack up and we get a new set of standards on the other end of it. The tobacco industry also had a lot of money and per capita smoking rates are the lowest since data's been collected. Maybe we'll all be on GLP-1 agonists and not so vulnerable to compulsive design.
